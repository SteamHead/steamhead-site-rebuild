A Model U.N. club for high school, except about global monetary policy. Where to start, especially as a non-economist? If you have a teacher's schedule, this could be overwhelming. Enter AI. Prompt by M. James, The Plan by R. ChatGPT Prompt:

in the style of a teacher writing a plan for an after school club, please list ten activities with titles and descriptions that students would do in an after school club based on Model United Nations (MUN) but called Model International Monetary Fund (MIMF).

In a MIMF club, students research and role-play as executives working to foster global monetary cooperation, secure financial stability, facilitate international trade, promote high employment and sustainable economic growth, and reduce poverty around the world.

The Plan

Here are ten potential activities that a MIMF club could do:

1.  "Understanding the Role of the IMF": This activity would involve students learning about the history, mission, and functions of the IMF. This could include reading background materials, watching videos, and engaging in discussions to gain a deeper understanding of the organization and its role in the global economy.
2.  "Country Case Studies": In this activity, students would research a specific country and its economy, including its history, economic indicators, and relationship with the IMF. Students could then present their findings to the group and discuss the challenges and opportunities facing the country.
3.  "MIMF Simulation": This activity would involve students role-playing as executives working at the IMF, addressing real-world economic issues and challenges through simulated negotiations and decision-making. This could include developing loan programs, providing technical assistance, and working to stabilize financial markets.
4.  "International Trade Negotiations": In this activity, students would simulate international trade negotiations between different countries, working to reach mutually beneficial agreements on issues such as tariffs, quotas, and trade barriers.
5.  "Macroeconomic Policy-Making": This activity would involve students learning about different macroeconomic policy tools that governments and central banks can use to address issues such as inflation, unemployment, and economic growth. Students could then discuss and debate the pros and cons of different policy options.
6.  "Sustainable Development Goals": In this activity, students would learn about the UN's Sustainable Development Goals and how the IMF works to support countries in achieving these goals. Students could brainstorm and present ideas for how the IMF could play a more proactive role in supporting sustainable development.
7.  "Financial Stability and Crisis Management": This activity would involve students learning about different financial crises that have occurred in the past, including the causes and impacts of these crises. Students could then discuss and brainstorm strategies for preventing and managing future financial crises.
8.  "IMF Reform": In this activity, students would research and discuss potential reforms to the IMF, including proposals to increase the representation of emerging economies and to better align the organization's policies with sustainable development goals.
9.  "Current Events in Global Economics": This activity would involve students keeping track of and discussing current events in the global economy, including topics such as trade tensions, currency valuations, and economic indicators.
10.  "Careers in International Economics": In this activity, students could research and learn about different career paths in international economics, including roles in the IMF, World Bank, and other international organizations. Students could also invite guest speakers to talk about their careers and experiences in the field.

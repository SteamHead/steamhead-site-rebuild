This week I read a research article written by Dr. Taylor of Western New Mexico University’s School of Education in which she discussed differentiated instruction (2015). Afterwards, I watched an old video of my student presenting her project at the end of a design class unit called “MakeFashion Edu” detailing the large choices she was able to make (Massey, 2020). I enjoyed the clear breakdown of categories, as I am personally a fan of using taxonomies to organize my thoughts on a subject, while acknowledging that they present just a model of realities found in classrooms.

I believe I can structure some aspects of my future teaching efforts around inclusivity, and use Dr. Taylor’s framework to inform some key points in “product” and “process”.

First, I would like to address Dr. Taylor’s discussion on “content”, which forms a strong leg of my lessons. The depth and breadth of my content has always been flexible, and I was pleased to see that Dr. Taylor advised on many of the things that I already focus on. Dr. Taylor advises employing several content strategies to to address different learning styles (2015), and I indeed offer videos, books in different languages, and graphic instructions that students may choose from.

However, when I reflect on the process, I found out that I felt both my processes and well as what Dr. Taylor wrote about are lacking. Dr. Taylor discussed using three different teaching processes, and then having her students reflect on the differences (2015, p. 16). I do the same, but I think what I could do differently is offer the students the choice of which teaching process to engage with, rather than having them reflect on the differences between several methods. The reflection component is important, but moving beyond that should be a real result of choosing your own learning style. I have many digital resources that could make this possible.

Lastly, Dr. Taylor’s “product” musings indicate she would allow students to present very different summative results to the teacher (2015, p. 14). Personally, I allow students to focus on very different aspects of their product, however I don’t allow different products entirely. For example, an electronic outfit might focus on either the electronic sensors or it might focus on the colors and shape of the garment, but both of the products are wearable garments. My student mentioned in her video that she felt overwhelmed by some choices (Massey, 2020), so it does make me consider how I might introduce even larger choices to the students. Dr. Taylor’s proposals sound scary to me, but my thoughts linger on them, and I wonder if students might be able to produce different kinds of work in the same class, or have greater levels of specialization. I will continue to reflect on this.

In conclusion, I I think inclusivity can be improved in my classroom by my offering broader choices, whereas previously I seem to have focused on choices of depth. This might be typical “teacher thinking”, where I allow varying “levels of success” without considering that success can come from entirely different sources.

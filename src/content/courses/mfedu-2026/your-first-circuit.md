Here's the whole lesson in one sentence: **take an LED and a coin cell battery, and make it light up.**

That's it. No instructions yet. Get a CR2032 battery and an LED, and figure it out.

## Seriously, go try it first

(We'll wait.)

...

Got light? Congratulations — you just built a **circuit**: a path for electrical current to flow from one side of a battery, through something useful, and back to the other side. Everything else in this module — paper circuits, wearable circuits, programmable LED strips — is this exact idea with better packaging.

Didn't light? Flip the LED around and try again. That flip is the actual lesson:

![Anatomy of an LED: the longer leg is the positive anode, the shorter leg is the negative cathode](/images/courses/mfedu-2026/led-anatomy.svg)

## What you discovered

- The LED's **longer leg is positive** (the *anode*); the **shorter leg is negative** (the *cathode*). Current only flows one way through it — that's the "D" in LED, *diode*.
- The battery has a **+** face and a **−** face. Positive leg to positive face, negative to negative. Backwards = nothing (harmlessly nothing — you can't hurt an LED with a coin cell by flipping it).

## Keep playing

- How many LEDs can you light on one battery? What happens to the brightness?
- Compare colors: blue and green LEDs are hungrier than red — mix them on one battery and sometimes the red hogs the power. Which combinations work?
- Pull out your **Elements Explorer** color words from the Design It module. Can you match "angry," "calm," "alive" with actual LEDs? You're now designing with light.

> **For teachers:** The sequencing here is the pedagogy: exploration *before* explanation, every time. Let designers light the LED, then ask what they noticed and why they think it works one way only — let them explain in their own words before you introduce "anode" or electron flow. The vocabulary lands ten times harder when it names something they already did.

## Before you move on

- Every designer on the team has personally lit an LED (not watched one get lit)
- You can explain long-leg/short-leg to someone who missed class

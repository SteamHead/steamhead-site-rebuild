Runway pieces eventually meet real fabric, and real fabric meets the sewing machine. Here's the MakeFashion Edu way in — built on a trick that removes the scariest part.

## Start with no thread

Meet the machine: presser foot, hand positions (fingers stay *outside* the foot's neighborhood), foot pedal. Then — **before any threading** — put paper with printed lines under the needle and drive. No thread means no tangles, no stakes, and a perforated line that shows exactly where you steered.

When your unthreaded lines stay on the road, learn to thread the machine, switch between straight stitch and zigzag, and run the paper again. You'll *see* your own improvement in the perforations — self-assessment built right into the paper.

Everything else — hems, seams, tension — we teach **just in time**: when your piece needs a technique, that's the day you learn it. Nobody ever mastered sewing from a lecture.

## Patchwork with a story

The perfect first real project: sew scraps into one piece of fabric (~14″ × 14″ makes a pillow). Patchwork isn't just for grandma's quilts — it's expressive, forgiving, and secretly a design exercise.

**Crazy patchwork** — random shapes, overlapped and zigzag-stitched along the edges. Chaotic, fast, and full of energy.

**Block patchwork** — 4″ × 4″ squares cut with a cardboard template, arranged in a deliberate pattern, sewn right-sides-together with a ¼″ seam, pairs into fours into rows.

Then ask the design question: **what story does your patchwork tell?** Where did the scraps come from? Were the colors chosen or found? Intentional pattern or beautiful accident? (Old t-shirts from your team's past events make patchwork that's *literally* made of story.)

## Cutting that behaves

- Fabric scissors cut **fabric only** — paper dulls them into bread knives. Label them; defend them.
- Flat surface, bottom blade sliding along the table, wide cuts, move forward after each cut, follow the line without leaning.

> **For teachers:** The no-thread paper stage is the confidence engine of this whole lesson — don't skip it even for students who claim experience. If you've never used a machine yourself, spend an evening with any beginner video tutorial first; you only need to stay one lesson ahead. Similar-weight fabrics make patchwork dramatically easier for beginners.

## Before you move on

- Straight and zigzag lines on paper, on the road, threaded
- A patchwork piece exists — and you can tell its story

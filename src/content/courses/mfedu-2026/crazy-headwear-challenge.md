Everything so far has been practice. This is the first **piece**: design and build headwear that tells a story. Hat, headband, crown, wig, scarf — anything worn on the head qualifies.

**The only rule: it must include a working circuit that lights up somewhere — and the light should be part of the story, not just decoration.**

## Why headwear first?

It's the perfect first build: small enough to finish, big enough to be seen across a room, structurally forgiving (nobody expects a hat to drape like a gown), and it sits at eye level — where runway audiences actually look.

## The process is the course in miniature

1. **Story first.** What is this headwear saying? One sentence, written down.
2. **Ideate on paper.** The head-and-shoulders croquis on your [Croquis sheet](/courses/mfedu-2026/worksheets/croquis-figures/) exists for exactly this. Three versions, then choose.
3. **Elements check.** Line, color, shape, texture — one deliberate decision each.
4. **Circuit plan on the sketch.** Battery hides where? Lights land where? Parallel, right?
5. **Build, fail, adjust, persist.** The gap between sketch and object is where making lives.

Raw materials: paper, cardboard, fabric scraps, recyclables, and whatever your imagination grabs — plus adhesives, cutting tools, and color. For inspiration (not for copying — be your own designer): [Instructables' silly hats gallery](https://www.instructables.com/contest/sillyhats/entries/).

## From our designers

Three students who took this challenge in wonderfully different directions:

> "My brother is a musician, so I wanted to make him a hat that showed this part of his personality." — **Victoria**

> "I believe you can be creative with whatever you have around, so I used scratch paper and fingernail polish to create this giant bow." — **Isabella**

> "I am a feathers, flowers and sparkles kind of girl, but that doesn't mean I am not strong too. The lights represent how my brain lights up when I get to be creative." — **Italia**

Notice Italia's line: *the lights represent something.* That's the standard.

> **For teachers:** Timebox the build to 1–2 sessions — scarcity fuels creativity here, and this is a warm-up, not the final piece. End with a two-minute mini runway or photo walk: every designer says the story, shows the light. It's a rehearsal for the exhibition module and a checkpoint for who needs circuit help before the big build.

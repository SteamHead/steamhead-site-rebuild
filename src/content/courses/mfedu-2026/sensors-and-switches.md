A switch makes the wearer part of the circuit. A sensor makes the *world* part of it. This is the deepest end of the course: pieces that respond.

## Start dumb (it's smarter)

Before any code, build a **mechanical switch** — two copper tape pads that touch when something moves:

- A **clasp switch**: bracelet closes, circuit closes, lights on.
- A **paperclip switch**: remember Sheriff Boxy from Trashion Fashion? A paperclip completing the circuit *is* a switch, and it shipped.
- A **pressure switch**: two pads with foam between them — lights that fire on a handshake, a step, a hug.

Mechanical switches are free, unbreakable by software, and teach the core idea: **input changes output.**

## Then let the board listen

Your micro:bit or Circuit Playground ships with senses: buttons, tilt/shake detection, light level, sound, temperature. Each one is a story trigger:

- **Shake** → the piece flares (anger, alarm, applause?)
- **Darkness** → the piece wakes up (what does your cause do when nobody's watching?)
- **Sound level** → lights climb with crowd noise (a piece the audience powers!)
- **Touch** → one LED of hope lights per handshake

Pick the sensor *after* you pick the sentence: "When ___ happens, my piece ___ — because the story says so."

## What this looks like at full power

**Razor Wings** — one of our favorite pieces ever walked: ask a rough-and-tumble student what she wants to build and you get wings with attitude, built to move. Read [the build story](/blog/razor-wings/). And for where sensing can ultimately go, our [NFC-powered lights experiment](/blog/nfc-powered-lights/) made garments respond to a tap of a phone.

Kiki's *Dual Emotions* from last lesson is the complete thought: sensor → emotion → light. That's the ceiling this course points at — and students reach it.

## The final challenge

Add **one** responsive element to your team's piece — mechanical or sensed — that a runway audience will actually notice at the pause. Then rehearse it: responsive pieces change the model's choreography (now the *walk* triggers the story).

> **For teachers:** Enforce the one-element rule ruthlessly; responsiveness is where scope creep eats teams alive the week before a show. The mechanical switch track means this lesson fully works with zero microcontrollers — sensors are the extension, not the requirement.

## Before the runway takes you

- Your piece responds to something, on purpose, reliably — ten times out of ten
- The response is in the artist statement, because it's part of the argument now

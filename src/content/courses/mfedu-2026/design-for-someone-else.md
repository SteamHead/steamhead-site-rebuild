Professional designers rarely design for themselves. They get a **client** — someone whose story they have to understand before a single sketch happens. Designing for someone else is the fastest empathy workout we know, and it reliably makes designers better at telling their *own* story afterward.

## Your client is waiting

Grab the **[Client Brief worksheet](/courses/mfedu-2026/worksheets/client-brief/)** and pick a client:

**The organic pizza shop.** All-natural ingredients, a storefront full of plants, and a menu of things customers have never tried. They need employee outfits that say *trust us, try something new, this is what healthy tastes like*.

**The desert racers.** A track team in the desert — brutal summer heat, cold dry winters, and a winning streak. Every runner loves the desert differently: some the animals, some the sunsets. One team identity, many stories inside it.

**The student with magical powers.** Proud of who they are, tired of being feared for it, always first to help. They don't want a superhero costume — they want the runway to understand that *differences aren't scary*.

**The trail guardian.** Leave No Trace is their motto; every weekend it's cleanups and trail repairs. How does devotion to nature look when it walks?

(Teachers and teams: invent your own clients freely — the best ones are drawn from people your students actually know.)

## The rule of reasons

Every element decision on the brief — color, line, shape, texture — needs a reason *drawn from the client's story*. This is the habit that separates design from decoration, and you'll need it fully installed by the time you defend your own piece at the exhibition.

## The test

Present your brief and sketch to another team *without naming your client*. If they can guess who it's for, your design is speaking. If they can't, find the element that's mumbling and turn it up.

> **For teachers:** Younger designers do best with the pizza shop and trail guardian; the magical-powers client opens deeper conversations about identity and belonging with older groups. Push every "because it looks cool" one step further: cool *how*? Cool for *whom*?

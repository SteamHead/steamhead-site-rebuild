A runway piece is a *published statement*. When your jacket says "109 people a day," someone in the audience is going to ask where that number came from — and "I saw it online somewhere" melts under gallery lighting.

Before anything goes on your piece, it goes through **SIFT**, media researcher Mike Caulfield's four moves for separating fact from fiction online:

![The SIFT method: Stop, Investigate the source, Find better coverage, Trace to the original](/images/courses/mfedu-2026/sift-method.svg)

## Three words you need

- **Misinformation** — wrong by accident: mislabeled photos, garbled statistics, satire taken seriously.
- **Disinformation** — wrong on purpose: fabricated content, manufactured rumors.
- **Malinformation** — *true* information deployed to harm.

Your advocacy piece can accidentally spread any of the three. SIFT is the filter.

## A real example, start to finish

Say you've heard that the fashion industry is *the second-largest polluter in the world*, and you want your Trashion piece to say so.

**Stop.** Where did you hear it? Everywhere, right? That's suspicious by itself — huge round claims that "everyone knows" are exactly the ones to check.

**Investigate & Find.** A search for "fashion industry pollution rank" turns up a New York Times piece reporting that the "second-largest" claim is *disputed* — nobody can trace it to a real study. Other coverage (The Guardian, EcoCult) agrees fashion is a major polluter, but ranks it differently.

**Trace.** The original source of "second largest"? It doesn't seem to exist.

**The design decision:** your piece drops the fake ranking and says what survives checking — fashion is a major polluter, and buying used beats buying fast fashion. Your argument just got *stronger*, because now nobody can dismiss it with "actually, that number's made up."

## Activity: Check your claims

Each team lists the 2–3 factual claims their cause depends on, then runs each through a **[SIFT Fact Check sheet](/courses/mfedu-2026/worksheets/sift-check/)**. Keep the sheets — they go in your exhibition display later as proof of process.

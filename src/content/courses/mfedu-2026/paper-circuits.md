A coin cell and an LED made light. Now we give the electricity somewhere to *go*: **copper tape** — sticky on one side, conductive everywhere — turns any sheet of paper into a circuit board you can fold, draw on, and eventually wear.

## The three circuits

![Three circuit types: simple, parallel, and series](/images/courses/mfedu-2026/circuit-types.svg)

- **Simple** — one path, one LED. Your proof of life.
- **Parallel** — every LED gets its own path. Brightest, and if one LED dies the rest keep going. **This is what runway pieces use** — you do not want one loose leg blacking out your whole finale.
- **Series** — one path through every LED. Educational, dim, and fragile: one failure kills the chain. Build it once to understand why we don't build it again.

## Build all three

Print the **[Paper Circuit Practice sheet](/courses/mfedu-2026/worksheets/circuit-practice/)** and lay copper tape along the paths.

The craft that makes it work:

1. **Peel a little backing at a time.** Pull the whole strip and it tangles instantly — everyone does this exactly once.
2. **Corners:** if your tape has conductive adhesive, tear and overlap freely. If not, make corners from one continuous piece — pull the tape the *wrong* way, then fold it back over itself in the right direction, so copper touches copper.
3. **LEDs do the splits:** bend the legs flat apart, long leg taped to the positive path, short leg to the negative, clear tape holding each leg *firmly* down.
4. **The battery bridges the gap** — positive path to its + face, negative path to its − face, and never both paths touching one face (that's a short circuit: all current, no light).

## When it doesn't light (it won't, at first)

Ninety percent of dead paper circuits are **a leg not pressing hard enough on the tape** — push it down. The rest: a hairline break in the tape, a sneaky short where + touches −, or non-conductive adhesive hiding between copper and leg (fold so copper touches copper).

Debugging is not the boring part before the project. Debugging **is** the skill.

> **For teachers:** Order copper tape with conductive adhesive if you can — it removes the trickiest craft step. Have teams answer the worksheet's removal experiment (pull one LED out of the parallel circuit, then the series one) out loud; it's the moment the difference stops being a diagram.

## Before you move on

- All three circuit types lit
- You fixed at least one dead circuit and can say what was wrong

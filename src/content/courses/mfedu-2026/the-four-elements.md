Every design decision you'll make on your piece comes down to four levers. Movements have used them for a century (remember suffrage white?); now they're yours.

## Line

Lines carry energy. Horizontal lines rest; verticals stand at attention; diagonals *move*; jagged lines argue. Look at a referee's shirt, a general's stripes, the racing stripe on a car — line is doing the talking. **With lights:** an LED strip is a line you can switch on. Where it points is part of your argument.

## Color

Color is the fastest signal you have — audiences read it before they read anything else. Red shouts, blue calms, green grows, gold announces. Colors are also *claimed*: pink ribbons, red shirts, rainbow stripes. Your cause may already have a color vocabulary; find out before you accidentally speak someone else's.

## Shape

Shape is the silhouette test: if your piece were a shadow, what would it say? Two stacked squares read *robot* before any detail arrives. A towering collar reads *power*. Wings read *freedom* — which is why they keep appearing on our runways.

## Texture

Texture is the element beginners skip and lighting designers obsess over — because **texture is what turns a bare LED into a glow**. Tulle diffuses light into a cloud. Foil bounces it into sparks. Fleece swallows it whole. When you pick fabric, you're picking what your lights will look like.

## Activity: Elements Explorer

Work through the **[Elements Explorer worksheet](/courses/mfedu-2026/worksheets/elements-explorer/)** — draw the lines, claim the colors, silhouette the shapes, and swatch real fabric for texture. Then the last section aims all four at *your* cause.

As you share, explain your choices. "There are no wrong answers" is true — but "no reasons" isn't allowed. *Pink because pink is friendly and we want newcomers to feel safe* is design. *Pink because I like pink* is a coin flip.

> **For teachers:** Bring a real scrap bin for the texture section — the difference between reading about diffusion and holding tulle in front of a phone flashlight is the whole lesson. Close with the discussion: what current movements are using these elements right now? Can the same four levers spread misinformation? (Connect back to SIFT.)

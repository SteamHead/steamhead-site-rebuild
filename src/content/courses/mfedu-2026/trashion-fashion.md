You SIFT-checked this back in Research It: the fashion industry is a major polluter — whatever its exact ranking. We refuse to advocate on a runway while adding to the problem, so MakeFashion Edu pieces are built from fabric remnants, thrifted garments, and upcycled materials. **Trashion Fashion takes that all the way: design and build a runway piece from materials that were headed for the trash.**

Accessory, footwear, jewelry, or full outfit — your call. It carries two stories at once: your team's cause, and reduce-reuse-recycle itself.

## Raid the recycling

Plastic bags, trash bags, bottles, wrappers, foil, cardboard, newspaper, old t-shirts, broken toys — all fair game. Two secrets the pros know:

- **Trash diffuses beautifully.** A grocery bag glows like a lantern; a water bottle turns one LED into a column of light. You learned texture-as-lighting in the Elements lesson — this is its moment.
- **Plastic bags can become fabric.** Fused with an iron, they turn into a surprisingly tough textile — [our how-to guide is here](/blog/how-to-makefashion-fuse-plastic-bags-to-create-fabric/).

## The process

1. What's your recycling story — and how does it braid with your team's cause? Write both down.
2. Croquis sketches, several; [Design Notes](/courses/mfedu-2026/worksheets/design-notes/) to think on paper.
3. Plan the circuit into the design (battery, lights, switch — parallel).
4. Gather trash. Let the materials talk back — the best Trashion ideas come *from* what you find.
5. Choose, build, adjust, redo, persist — until it becomes what you envisioned.
6. Celebrate and show it off.

> **For teachers:** Start collecting materials a week early (a classroom "trashion bin" fills itself once announced). This build deserves more time than the headwear warm-up — 3–5 sessions. Mid-build, run a two-minute stand-up per team: story, progress, current obstacle. It keeps the persist step honest.

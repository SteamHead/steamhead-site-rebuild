Sharing the work is not the victory lap after the learning — it **is** part of the learning. When young designers see strangers genuinely curious about what they made, something shifts: they answer questions, defend choices, explain failures, and walk away more confident and more willing to persist on the next hard thing. We've watched it happen at every show since the first one.

**Exhibitions are celebrations of success.**

## What goes on display

The process is as important as the product, so a MakeFashion Edu display shows the journey:

- Design sketches (yes, the rejected versions too)
- Work-in-progress photos
- The finished piece — or its photo, if it's being modeled live
- The **artist statement**: names and ages of the design team, the model, materials and technology, and above all *the story the piece argues*

Now finish what you started in Sketch to Story: take that draft paragraph and polish it on the **[Artist Statement sheet](/courses/mfedu-2026/worksheets/artist-statement/)**. Your SIFT fact-checks belong in the display too — receipts make advocacy credible.

## Gallery, not science fair

Think about how an art gallery treats work, and give your designers the same respect:

- **Uniform statement cards** across all displays — the gallery reads clean and professional
- **Room to move** between displays
- **No long-table poster lineups** — that's a science fair, and this isn't one
- **Mind the lighting**: your pieces glow. Where can the room go dim?

Plan the whole thing on the **[Exhibition Plan sheet](/courses/mfedu-2026/worksheets/exhibition-plan/)** — location (cafeteria, library, hallway, community space), traffic flow, power, and whether a runway show anchors it.

Can't gather in person? A virtual gallery is a real gallery — we ran them through the pandemic and wrote up [how to start one](/blog/virtual-maker-exhibition-how-to-start-educator-guide-part-1/).

## Activity: The question drill

Designers and models will be asked questions — that's the point of exhibiting. Drill the big three in pairs until the answers come without reading:

1. *What's the story?* (under a minute, from the heart)
2. *How does it work?* (circuit words you earned in Light It Up)
3. *What would you do differently?* (the honest answer is the impressive one)

> **For teachers:** The question drill is the most valuable hour of this module — communication skill gains here transfer everywhere. Invite an audience that isn't just parents: other classes, admin, the local paper. Strangers' questions hit different, in the best way.
